\section*{S1. Fixed-time reference models}

\noindent For Section 6.1, the models were evaluated at 30, 50, 70 and 90\% of each test engine's life in the same outer folds. They were trained on the fit engines (75\% of each outer training part); stacking and the calibration shifts of Fig. 2 used only the calibration engines (the other 25\%). The eleven models at half of life are: age only; an input--output hidden Markov model; the similarity model; gradient-boosted trees; a temporal convolutional network with and without augmentation by a conditional generative adversarial network; a hybrid Transformer with an engineered-feature branch; the convolutional and LSTM networks; a physics-informed Bayesian exponential-degradation model with a population prior in the style of Gebraeel et al. [21]; and a stacked hybrid of ten base models and the cycle count, whose quantile stacker and shift were learned on calibration engines pooled over life stages.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Age only}: the mean remaining life of the training engines that survived beyond the current cycle.
\item \textbf{Gradient-boosted trees}: histogram gradient boosting with absolute-error loss (400 iterations, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, minimum 40 samples per leaf, no target cap) on features of the seven temperature and speed sensors (exponential mean with span 5, expanding level, slope and standard deviation, a pooled health index, early-life baseline and age).
\item \textbf{Input--output hidden Markov model}: three health states and an absorbing failure state, four discrete symbols from a principal-component quantisation, one transition matrix per operating regime; remaining life = expected time to absorption.
\item \textbf{LSTM and CNN}: as in Section 5.4 of the main text.
\item \textbf{Similarity}: principal-component trajectories matched to all training trajectories over a 60-cycle window; the remaining lives of the 30 closest matches are kernel-weighted into a distribution.
\item \textbf{Physics-informed Bayesian model}: a linear health index with a path a + exp($\theta$ + $\beta$t) and a failure level D, with a multivariate normal population prior; posterior by importance sampling (20,000 samples) conditioned on survival.
\item \textbf{Hybrid Transformer, TCN, TCN with GAN augmentation, stacked hybrid}: quantile models as described in the reproduction package.
\end{itemize}

\section*{S2. Proof of Proposition 1}

\noindent If $\varepsilon$ has distribution \textit{F} not depending on \textit{s}, the warning is correct if and only if \textit{a} $\le$ $\varepsilon$ + \textit{s} $\le$ \textit{b}, that is, $\varepsilon$ $\in$ [\textit{a} \textminus{} \textit{s}, \textit{b} \textminus{} \textit{s}], an interval of width \textit{w} = \textit{b} \textminus{} \textit{a}. Hence \textit{P}(\textit{s}) = \textit{F}(\textit{b} \textminus{} \textit{s}) \textminus{} \textit{F}(\textit{a} \textminus{} \textit{s}) $\le$ sup\textsubscript{x} [\textit{F}(\textit{x} + \textit{w}) \textminus{} \textit{F}(\textit{x})] = \textit{C}\textsubscript{w}(\textit{F}), which is part (i).

\noindent For part (ii), let \textit{f} be the density, unimodal and symmetric about \textit{m}, and write \textit{g}(\textit{x}) = \textit{F}(\textit{x} + \textit{w}) \textminus{} \textit{F}(\textit{x}). Then \textit{g}'(\textit{x}) = \textit{f}(\textit{x} + \textit{w}) \textminus{} \textit{f}(\textit{x}). For \textit{x} < \textit{m} \textminus{} \textit{w}/2 the point \textit{x} + \textit{w} is closer to \textit{m} than \textit{x}, so \textit{f}(\textit{x} + \textit{w}) $\ge$ \textit{f}(\textit{x}) and \textit{g} is non-decreasing; for \textit{x} > \textit{m} \textminus{} \textit{w}/2 the reverse holds. Therefore \textit{g} is maximal at \textit{x} = \textit{m} \textminus{} \textit{w}/2, and \textit{g}(\textit{m} \textminus{} \textit{w}/2) = Pr(|$\varepsilon$ \textminus{} \textit{m}| $\le$ \textit{w}/2). The interval [\textit{a} \textminus{} \textit{s}, \textit{b} \textminus{} \textit{s}] is centred at \textit{m} when \textit{s}\textsuperscript{*} = (\textit{a} + \textit{b})/2 \textminus{} \textit{m}; for [\textminus{}13, +2] this is \textminus{}5.5 \textminus{} \textit{m}. \hfill$\blacksquare$

\noindent The proposition concerns a fixed distribution. In the sequential policy, a different shift moves the alarm to a different cycle, where the unshifted error has a different distribution; Section 6.5 of the main text measures this. The submitted version compared the realised in-window rate with the concentration Pr(|$\varepsilon$ \textminus{} \textit{m}| $\le$ 7.5) computed from the same test alarms; since both come from the same alarms, their agreement does not test the assumption, and that comparison has been removed.

\section*{S4. Leakage checks and preprocessing audit}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item (E1) outer and inner unit sets are disjoint in every fold;
\item (E2) causality: for 15 test engines and six random cut-offs each, predictions from truncated histories equal those from full histories;
\item (E3) a negative control at \textit{H} = 10 in outer fold 1 trained on remaining-life labels built from lives permuted across engines;
\item (E4) refitting fold 1 with its stored settings reproduces the stored outcome of each of its test engines at \textit{H} = 10.
\end{itemize}

\noindent All four checks passed on PHM08: disjoint sets (E1); a maximum absolute difference of 0 over 90 truncation checks (E2); permuted lives reduced the fold-1 in-window rate at \textit{H} = 10 from 97.7\% to 15.9\% (E3); exact reproduction of fold 1 (E4).

\noindent For this revision every preprocessing step was checked for use of test units. Regime z-scores and the removal of constant sensors use the training (or fit) units of each split; regimes are obtained by rounding the operating settings, without fitting; the N-CMAPSS channels were fixed in the protocol; bearing condition statistics come from training bearings, pooled when a condition has no training bearing; the threshold grid of the health-indicator alarm, the constant of the constant-estimate control and the capacity thresholds come from training values. The one dependency found was the block length $\kappa$ of the battery and bearing data (Section 5.2 of the main text), which has been corrected, together with a backward fill of missing per-cycle battery values within a cell.

\section*{S5. Battery analyses}

\noindent \textbf{Data curation (fixed before any model was run).} Structural checks required three deviations, recorded below the hashed section of the protocol. (D1) In the first and third batches the cycle at which capacity falls below 80\% of nominal is not in the summary, so a record counts as complete if it runs to the cycle before. (D2) Five cells of the first batch were continued as five cells of the second; the ten records were identified from the providers' loading code and excluded. (D3) Five cells with a finite official life never fall below 0.91 Ah in the cleaned data and were excluded as censored. Two further records have no official cycle life in the providers' cell list and were not used. This leaves 123 cells (36, 43 and 44 from the three batches). A secondary analysis without four channels flagged as noisy by the providers uses 119 cells. The batches were started in May 2017, June 2017 and April 2018, used different rest periods and share almost no charging protocol (of the 41 protocols of the second batch, 2 occur in another batch); their median lives are 772, 481 and 1006 cycles.

\section*{S6. N-CMAPSS: secondary results}

\noindent At \textit{H} = 10 and 15 the recipe reached 74.7\% and 70.7\%. On the official split (39 test engines) it reached 22/39 = 56.4\% at \textit{H} = 20, compared with 43.6\% for the MLP with Kalman filter and 30.8\% for age-only. The median error at \textit{H} = 20 was \textminus{}2.7 cycles; the debounce selected in every fold was \textit{k} = 5. Two deviations are recorded: seed 106 was used for the inner split of the official split, which has no fold number, and the protocol calls the Kalman filter of the MLP competitor constant-velocity, whereas it is the scalar filter with a fixed decrease of one cycle per cycle. In an exploratory re-run with all 14 channels, the rate at \textit{H} = 20 was 65.7\%.

\section*{S7. Bearing analyses}

\noindent The 17 bearings ran under condition 1 (1800 rpm, 4000 N; seven bearings), condition 2 (1650 rpm, 4200 N; seven) and condition 3 (1500 rpm, 5000 N; three). Lives ranged from 230 to 2803 snapshots (median 1428, about 4 h). \textbf{Protocol analysis} ($\kappa$ = 7 from all bearings): 0 of 17 first warnings in the window at \textit{H} = 20 (7 too early, 10 missed); 0 of 11 on the official split. \textbf{Corrected analysis} ($\kappa$ from training bearings: 7, 4, 9, 6, 7 in the five folds, 4 on the official split): 0 of 17 in cross-validation (7 too early, 0 late, 10 missed), 1 of 11 on the official split; age-only 1 and the MLP with Kalman filter 0 of 17 in cross-validation. Folds with $\kappa$ = 7 reproduced the protocol results exactly. With the physical task fixed (\textit{H} = 140 snapshots, window [\textminus{}91, +14] snapshots), $\kappa$ $\in$ \{3, 5, 7, 10, 14\} gave 1, 0, 0, 0, 1 correct first warnings of 17. The pre-specified theory-guided shift (STW-T) gave 1 of 17 in the corrected analysis.

\noindent Table S4 lists the bearings with a diagnosis made after the run with an ad hoc criterion: the number of blocks ($\kappa$ = 7) before failure from which the summed RMS of the two channels stayed above 1.5 times its mean over the first 20 blocks (0: never sustained; 1: only the final block). In 11 of 17 bearings the rise came within the last six blocks (about seven minutes). This is consistent with the hypothesis that, with these features, most failures gave too little notice for a 20-block alarm, but it does not explain the misses and very early alarms on bearings with long degradation; small numbers (13--14 training bearings per fold), three operating conditions and the feature set are competing explanations, and none was tested.

\section*{S9. Thirty random 50-engine test sets}

\noindent With 50 random test and 168 training engines, drawn thirty times on PHM08 (seeds 1001--1030; settings re-selected inside the training engines), the number of correct first warnings per 50 engines at \textit{H} = 20 averaged 45.9 (91.8\%, standard deviation 3.3 points, range 42--49); on average 2.1 alarms per 50 were late and 2.0 too early, and no engine was missed. At least 48 of 50 were correct in 4 of 30 iterations. The iterations share engines, so they are not independent samples; they indicate the variability of a single 50-engine test, including refitting and selection.
