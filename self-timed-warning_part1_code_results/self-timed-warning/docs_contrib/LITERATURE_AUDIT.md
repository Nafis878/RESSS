# Contribution and prior-work audit

Search date: 7 October 2026. Primary publisher pages and original papers were checked. This is a targeted novelty audit, not an exhaustive systematic review. Subscription papers below were assessed from publisher abstracts/references; their full methods were not accessible.

| Prior work | Established overlap | Possible remaining distinction |
|---|---|---|
| [Angelopoulos et al., Conformal Risk Control, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.02814v4) | Finite-sample expected risk control for monotone losses, including binary losses, calibration correction, and extensions. CAT's binary loss is failure before a planned replacement. Its order-statistic guarantee is a specialization. | The alarm-specific critical score and lead/grid mapping are domain constructions, not a new risk-control theorem. |
| [Tan et al., Conformal Lesion Segmentation, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17897) | Computes a critical threshold per calibration example and calibrates a quantile of those thresholds for a target loss. | Different application: CAT scores an entire degradation trajectory before its latest admissible action. The critical-threshold calibration pattern itself predates this work. |
| [Kumar, Nayak and Pati, 2026](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-29501-9_23) | Predictive maintenance with conformal calibration, a constrained safe threshold region and economic threshold optimization. Online 23 June 2026. | Their target is false-alarm rate; CAT's target is one unplanned failure per run-to-failure unit, incorporating lead time and decision grid. Full-text comparison remains necessary before claiming a first method. |
| [Almeida et al., 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14759217261445819) | Maintenance cost minimization under an explicit miss-rate constraint, probabilistic calibration and split conformal prediction. Online 19 May 2026. | Bearing fault diagnosis/selective classification differs from CAT's alarm timing on a full trajectory. Full-text comparison remains necessary. |
| [Robinson, 2026](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/view/4724) | Gradient boosting and asymmetric conformalized quantile RUL intervals on all four C-MAPSS subsets. | Interval miscoverage is distinct from replacement failure after a trajectory-level first alarm. The mere use of conformal calibration for RUL is not new. |
| [Kamariotis et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03759v2) | Decision-oriented maintenance metric M, probability-based policies and cost-driven selection. | This repository provides a matched-information empirical audit, grid/lead effects, transfer tests and threshold fragility checks. Reimplementation must not be described as inventing M or these policies. |

The generic claims “new distribution-free failure guarantee,” “first conformal predictive-maintenance method,” and “first cost optimization inside a conformal safe region” are not defensible from this audit. The ratio influence interval is a standard delta-method calculation and did not improve rare-failure coverage in the saved study.

A defensible candidate contribution is an alarm-trajectory formulation and reproducible cross-domain assessment of when calibration, threshold selection, observation time, lead time and decision grids change operational failure and cost. Establishing this as a new maintenance-specific method requires both a correct independent calibration design and a fuller comparison with the two 2026 maintenance papers. Empirical performance cannot establish literature novelty by itself.

Phase 0 candidate assessment: CAT is the candidate to finish because its trajectory score directly addresses the operational target. The paired M interval belongs in the supplement as a negative coverage finding. Adding another regressor or renaming a conformal quantile would not address the identified novelty gap.
