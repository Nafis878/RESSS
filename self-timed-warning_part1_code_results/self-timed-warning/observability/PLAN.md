# Pivot: paired maintenance-observability benchmark

Date: 8 October 2026. This is a post hoc research pivot, not preregistration. Preserve every original and RAMPE artifact. New sources: `observability/`; outputs: `out/observability/`.

The intended contribution is an evaluation resource and project-specific empirical result, not a new ensemble, RL method, survival estimator or first-ever censoring theorem. Priority for a scientific benchmark remains subject to a targeted prior-art audit and external review; an absent keyword match cannot prove novelty.

Question: How much of a competing maintenance policy's observed full-lifetime performance remains measurable after logging a different preventive-replacement policy? Keep the four existing policies, all eight evaluation regimes, all leads/costs and all logger-target pairs. Do not introduce favorable candidate policies. Full-life costs form a sealed evaluator truth table; user-facing evaluator inputs contain only logging endpoints and counterfactual outcomes measurable from their prefixes.

Observation model: removal does not change the pre-removal trajectory; units are independent replays of original run-to-end records, not deployed causal-intervention data. A logger failure reveals the complete original record. A logger preventive removal hides everything after its removal endpoint H. A competing policy is measurable if it completes at or before H, or if the logger failed. Pending alarms are not exposed here: outcome-summary replay is deliberately coarser than raw sensor-prefix replay. Outcome masks are generated from sealed truth, but the public view only exposes the resulting prefix-measurable status and observed outcomes. No target future life, RUL-at-alarm or failure label is released for unresolved rows.

Unknown target outcomes obey cost in {1,rho}, use>H (continuous time allowed) and use≤a declared cap. Caps 2000/5000/10000 are sensitivity assumptions for capped evaluation policies, not learned sample maxima or physical lifetime bounds. Existing observed policy outcomes must be below each cap or that configuration is rejected. Since every sampled target outcome is below a cap, clipping does not change this sample's truth. A valid decision certificate is only finite-cohort under these outcome constraints, not future population safety or a sharp result for a fully known sensor/degradation process.

Prove the **sharp identification interval in this coarsened outcome model** by extremizing unresolved costs and durations jointly. Its upper endpoint uses all unknown costs=rho and durations approaching H from above; lower endpoint uses costs=1 and duration=cap. With n_unknown=0, the interval collapses to the recorded rate. Failure interval is [observed failures/n,(observed failures+n_unknown)/n]. These are algebraic partial-identification results; do not claim they are novel general mathematics. Coupled risk/rate feasible-region refinements are future work, not hidden completed experiments.

Compare finite-cohort rate certification, complete-case deletion, unresolved-as-healthy substitution, and an explicitly leaky full-lifetime oracle. For every pair, report observable fraction, exact-risk interval, rate interval, true rate, false certification and missed measurable gains. True logger rate is observable. Certify target rate strictly below logger only if upper<logger; certify risk≤.05 only if upper risk≤.05. No asymptotic or conformal guarantee is claimed. Vary observation budget by releasing uniformly sampled unresolved outcomes from a sealed evaluator at fractions0/.1/.25/.5/1 and ten seeds2026–2035. This is retrospective extra-information sensitivity, not feasible or free physical audits of removed equipment.

Construct adversarial outcome completions with the same public view, one making target cheap/safe and another expensive/unsafe. These demonstrate nonidentification without claiming to reproduce fixed-policy sensor trajectories. An equality-preserving hidden-truth mutation test must show evaluator intervals and certifications remain unchanged.

Unit outcomes and split settings repeat across cost/lead/logger/target tasks. Report all settings and aggregate descriptively; never count them as independent units. Fixed source models do not imply a new retraining study. Broad conformal/survival/censored-learning ideas are existing prior art. After implementation search again for paired censoring benchmark, outcome observability masks, identification intervals and renewal-rate policy evaluation.
