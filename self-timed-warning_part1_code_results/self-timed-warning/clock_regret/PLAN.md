# Exact relative-regret selection from censored replacement records

8 October 2026. New research direction, specified before implementation runs. This is not preregistration: earlier failures and the observability findings motivated it. Preserve every prior artifact, including the complete method validation. Write only `clock_regret/` and `out/clock_regret/`.

Investigate a **problem-specific exact algorithm**, not a new general minimax principle. Input: independent finite-cohort replay records with integer logger endpoint H_i, indicator of logger failure, and K positive integer replacement ages fixed independently of these logged outcomes. A logger failure reveals lifetime L_i=H_i; preventive removal implies only L_i>=H_i+1. Policy k has use min(L_i,d_k), failure 1{L_i<=d_k}, and cost 1+(rho-1)*failure. All policies share the same latent lifetime for an asset. Costs have one common integer ratio rho>1. No lifetime cap, survival law, imputation, missing-at-random assumption, or future alarm is an algorithm input. This formulation is for fixed replacement ages or already committed deadlines, not arbitrary future sensor-triggered policies.

Target: choose k minimizing sup_L [rate_k(L)/min_j rate_j(L)] over all compatible integer lifetime completions. This is finite-cohort multiplicative regret, not mean unitwise cost/use, long-run population regret or future safety. Include self-comparisons exactly equal to one.

Hypothesized reduction: for an earlier candidate age a<b, only shared failures at the smallest compatible times or joint survival need be considered; at a fixed number of shared failures, sort logger horizons to select the smallest. For a later age a>b, worst clocks are the earlier deadline for shared failures, the earliest allowed intermediate failure for later-only failures, or joint survival. Enumerate their two counts; choose the largest exposure reductions subject to those counts. This should give an exact O(K^2*(n^2+n log n)) solver with explicit admissible adversarial lifetime witnesses. Prove before claiming correctness. Compare with exhaustive full-clock enumeration and a generic endpoint-state optimizer on small problems. Do not call the reduction novel until its precise contribution survives the literature audit.

Baselines using identical public information: (1) independent **sharp individual-policy** intervals with rectangular relative-regret upper bounds, diagonal fixed to one; (2) smallest sharp upper cost rate, earliest-age tie break; (3) complete-case cost rate as a diagnostic with earliest-age fallback if no labels. Independent marginal endpoints must respect known replacement ages and integer clocks; do not compare only with the looser earlier cap-based bound. The exact worst-case score of every baseline-selected policy is evaluated with the same coupled solver. Any guaranteed score reduction is an optimality result, not a significance claim.

Controlled experiments: 2,400 independent synthetic cohorts: n in {12,24}, logging age in {6,10}, rho in {5,20}, lifetime laws short Uniform integers4..14, long Uniform integers18..40, and 50/50 mixture of those laws. Ages are fixed {8,12,16,24}; 100 independently generated cohorts per24 setting cells, deterministic documented seed mapping. No law informs the algorithm. Compare realized relative regret with the rectangle selector and upper-rate selector using independent-cohort two-sided sign tests (ties discarded), one family of48 tests with Holm correction. Report means, paired bootstrap intervals, wins/ties/losses, all settings, and negative results. Do not infer superiority from deterministic optimality or use hidden lifetime labels for selection.
