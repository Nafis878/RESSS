# Prior-art audit: ordered shared-lifetime relative regret

Audit date: 8 October 2026. Verdict: **a specific computational contribution is plausible; first-in-literature priority is not established.** This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a novelty certificate. Earlier CAT, RAMPE and observability audits remain frozen; this audit concerns the new fixed-age solver.

## Precise claim being tested

An exact O(K^2(n^2+n log n)) arithmetic-operation algorithm for finite-cohort minimax multiplicative cost-rate regret among fixed integer replacement ages from right-censored lifetime records. All policies share each asset's latent lifetime. Ordered failure phases, sorted exposure gains and one or two failure counts yield sharp pairwise maxima and attaining lifetime witnesses, without a physical lifespan cap or a fitted lifetime distribution.

The candidate algorithmic contribution is Theorems 1 and 2 in [THEORY.md](THEORY.md). The finite maximum/supremum interchange, minimax principle, age-replacement cost model, missing-outcome uncertainty and fractional objectives are not claimed as new. Neither a new name nor a new application establishes priority.

## Search record and limits

Before implementing the final reduction, searches covered censored policy evaluation, maintenance regret, nonparametric replacement and shared failure clocks. After deriving the actual formulas, searches used: `"age replacement" "relative regret"`, `"replacement" "shared" "lifetime" "minimax regret"`, `"censored" "replacement" "minimax"`, finite-cohort replacement optimization, robust renewal ratios, shared-lifetime policy comparison, sorted adversarial scenarios, and min-max relative fractional optimization. Primary publisher pages, author manuscripts, official proceedings and arXiv were preferred. Inaccessible or abstract-only works are identified above. Search hits, publication metadata or a paper's abstract alone cannot establish that its complete development lacks the same result.

No inspected primary source was found to state the same finite-cohort censored-rate problem together with the two ordered failure-count formulas and their witnesses. That observation supports a **candidate** narrow contribution. It does not establish first discovery, exhaustive originality or the absence of an equivalent result under different notation. Reliability, robust fractional programming and scheduling literature are large; an independent specialist review and citation-chain search remain material requirements before a definitive novelty claim.

## Attempts to invalidate the mechanism

1. The general endpoint-state optimizer reproduces every audited optimum. Therefore claim a specialized exact reduction and operation bound, not an unprecedented capacity to solve the problem.
2. Individually sharp marginal bounds are computable from the same records. Their rectangular comparison differs strictly in an explicit example; the result is not merely the old box LP renamed.
3. Nested observation and common potential outcomes occur in prior work. The candidate distinction must reside in the count formulas for the nonlinear cohort-rate quotient.
4. A proof of correctness is not a proof of historical originality. P-values test performance contrasts, not originality.
5. Average cost and failure-risk results include losses. The robust criterion needs a decision maker who actually values its completion-set guarantee.

**Outcome B: POTENTIALLY NOVEL BUT INSUFFICIENTLY ESTABLISHED.** The exact algorithm is a substantive addition to this project; its external scientific priority remains unestablished. The answer to the strict question "Is a genuinely novel method already established?" remains **NO** at this evidence standard.
